# 5. The Hubble Tension as a Signal of Cosmological Phase Transition

## 5.1. The Standard Problem

In the ΛCDM model, the Hubble constant $H_0$ is a global parameter, derivable from either:

- The cosmic microwave background (CMB) via Planck (assuming ΛCDM up to redshift $z \sim 1100$), yielding $H_0 = 67.4 \pm 0.5 \, \text{km/s/Mpc}$;

- Local measurements (standard candles: Cepheids + SN Ia) via SH0ES, yielding $H_0 = 73.0 \pm 1.0 \, \text{km/s/Mpc}$.

The discrepancy of $\sim 5\sigma$ is known as the Hubble tension. In ΛCDM, this is attributed to unidentified systematic errors or new dark components (e.g., dark energy early-time).

---

## 5.2. Alternative Hypothesis: Phase-Dependent $G_{\text{eff}}$

If gravity emerges as an elastic response of a coherent fermionic medium (SPU), then the effective gravitational "constant" is not universal, but depends on the average surface density of the universe in the epoch considered:

- **Primordial epoch** ($z \gg 1$): high density → saturated medium → rigid regime → $G_{\text{eff}}^{\text{early}} < G_{\text{local}}$.

- **Local epoch** ($z \lesssim 0.1$): low density → elastic/coherent medium → $G_{\text{eff}}^{\text{late}} > G_{\text{eff}}^{\text{early}}$.

Since

$$H_0 \propto G_{\text{eff}} \rho$$

an increase in $G_{\text{eff}}$ at low redshift implies a higher value of $H_0$ measured locally.

## 5.3. Connection with the SPARC Threshold

The same transition observed in SPARC at $\Sigma_{\text{crit}} \approx 9.6 \times 10^3 \, M_\odot/\text{kpc}^2$ can be reinterpreted on a cosmological scale:

- Define a cosmological effective surface density:

$$\Sigma_{\text{cosmo}}(z) \equiv \frac{c \, H(z)}{2\pi G}$$

(dimensionally equivalent to a column of mass per unit area).

- At decoupling redshift ($z \sim 1100$), $\Sigma_{\text{cosmo}} \gg \Sigma_{\text{crit}}$ → braking regime (reduced gravity).

- Today ($z = 0$), $\Sigma_{\text{cosmo}} \ll \Sigma_{\text{crit}}$ → thrust regime (amplified gravity).

This regime change quantitatively explains why:

- The CMB fit (based on weak gravity) underestimates $H_0$.

- Local measurements (strong gravity) overestimate it relative to the standard model.
