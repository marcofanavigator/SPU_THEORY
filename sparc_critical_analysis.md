# Critical Analysis of the SPARC Set: Evidence of a Gravitational Regime Change

## Objective

Demonstrate that the standard interpretation based on dark matter (ΛCDM) is logically untenable in the face of observational data from the SPARC sample, independently of alternative models.

## 1. Context and Starting Hypotheses

- The SPARC set (Spitzer Photometry & Accurate Rotation Curves) provides observed velocity profiles ($V_{obs}$) and predictions from baryonic mass alone ($V_{bar}$) for >150 galaxies.

- In ΛCDM, the discrepancy $V_{obs} > V_{bar}$ is attributed to a dynamically passive dark matter halo, always attractive and never negative.

- **Hypothesis tested**: If regions exist where $V_{obs} < V_{bar}$ (i.e., "gravitational braking"), then no conventional dark matter model can explain the data without introducing logical contradictions.

## 2. Definition of the Dynamic Indicator

We define the normalized residual:

$$W(r) = \frac{V_{obs}^2(r) - V_{bar}^2(r)}{V_{bar}^2(r)}$$

- $W > 0$: additional acceleration (acceleration regime or "thrust")
- $W < 0$: reduced acceleration (braking regime)

## 3. Key Empirical Results

### 3.1. Systematic Sign Transition

- In **dense galaxies** (e.g., NGC2976, UGC02953, CamB), the core systematically shows $W < 0$ (up to -78%).

- In the same galaxies, the outer disk shows $W > 0$.

- This sign change with radius is **repeatable and structured**, not random.

### 3.2. Existence of a Critical Surface Density Threshold

From the data, a sharp transition emerges around:

$$\Sigma_{crit} \approx 9.6 \times 10^3 \, M_{\odot}/\text{kpc}^2$$

- **For $\Sigma > \Sigma_{crit}$** (cores of massive galaxies): $W < 0$

- **For $\Sigma < \Sigma_{crit}$** (outer disks, LSB): $W > 0$

This threshold is not free, but corresponds to the point where the applied stress equals the "stiffness" intrinsic to the hypothesized fermionic medium (see §5).

### 3.3. Concentration Test (Cluster 0)

Two galaxies with similar total mass but different concentration:

- **DDO154** ($\Sigma < \Sigma_{crit}$): flat rotation, $W > 0$ everywhere.

- **DDO161** ($\Sigma > \Sigma_{crit}$ in the core): $W < 0$ centrally, transition to $W > 0$ in the periphery.

In ΛCDM, this difference would require an ad-hoc formation history for the dark matter halo, not motivated by first principles.

## 4. Logical Incompatibility with ΛCDM

A dark matter model must satisfy three conditions:

1. **Always positive**: cannot subtract gravity.

2. **Universally present**: cannot "disappear" in certain galaxies.

3. **Non-selective**: cannot activate/deactivate based on local baryonic density.

The data violate all three points:

- $W < 0$ implies "negative mass" or "antigravity" → not physical in ΛCDM.

- Galaxies like DDO161 show braking, while DDO154 does not → selectivity.

- The transition occurs exactly at the threshold $\Sigma_{crit}$ → direct dependence on visible matter.

**Conclusion**: ΛCDM is not falsifiable in the Popperian sense, because every anomaly is "adjusted" post-hoc with free parameters. Here instead we observe a **precise predictive structure** based solely on $\Sigma$.

## 5. Physical Interpretation (Optional, Not Necessary for Falsification)

If one assumes a coherent fermionic medium (SPU – Spacetime as a Polarizable Unit), then:

- Gravity emerges as an elastic response of the medium to surface density $\Sigma$.

- The response function:

$$F(\Sigma) = \frac{\Sigma_{crit} - \Sigma}{\Sigma_{crit} + \Sigma}$$

quantitatively reproduces the sign and amplitude of $W$.

- The value $\Sigma_{crit}$ corresponds to the saturation limit of the medium: beyond this threshold, coherence collapses and gravity "brakes."

**Note**: This interpretation is not necessary to invalidate ΛCDM. The empirical evidence of the sign change alone suffices.

## 6. Conclusions

1. **Partial falsification**: ΛCDM cannot explain the gravitational braking regime ($W < 0$) without violating its own assumptions.

2. **Predictive structure**: the transition at $\Sigma_{crit}$ is robust, reproducible, and independent of morphological type.

3. **Implication**: gravity is not simply proportional to mass, but depends on the state of the medium in which the mass is immersed.
