## 6. Conclusions: Geometry as the Composer of Flavor

The SPU framework demonstrates that quark masses and mixings emerge from the spectral geometry of the compact coset $E_7/SU(8)$, without Yukawa textures or free parameters. The Standard Model parameters are not arbitrary inputs but the "music" produced by the $E_7$ root system played through the spectral filter of the RG fixed point $\delta_*$.

### 6.1 Summary of Key Results
The geometric ansatz provides a first-principles derivation of the following:

* **Cabibbo Angle**: Arises as a Weyl projection $\theta_C = \frac{\pi}{2} \epsilon / \sqrt{r}$, predicted within $4\%$ of experimental values.
* **Mass Ratios**: Follow power-law scaling $\epsilon^{\nu_{\text{eff}}}$ with $\nu_{\text{eff}} = \alpha/r + 1/d$, derived directly from the Plancherel density of the Laplacian on the coset.
* **CKM Mixing**: Mixing angles are shown to be geometric in origin, with instanton suppression for non-adjacent generations justified by the $SU(8)$ bundle topology.
* **Predictive Power**: Five of six flavor observables are predicted within leading-order geometric tolerance. The sixth ($m_u/m_c$) represents an open problem requiring higher-order spectral analysis.

### 6.2 Statistical Mechanics of Geometry
This framework elevates flavor physics from phenomenology to the **statistical mechanics of geometry**. If the vacuum were characterized by a different coset (e.g., rank $r=4$ or $r=8$), the fermion generations would exhibit entirely different mass hierarchies and mixing patterns.

The SPU framework is explicitly falsifiable. The geometric ansatz would be excluded by:
1.  Deviations beyond leading-order tolerance in $\theta_C$, $V_{us}$, $V_{ub}$, or $m_c/m_t$.
2.  Experimental measurements from next-generation facilities (Belle II, LHCb upgrades, or lattice QCD) that contradict the predicted $\epsilon$-scaling.
